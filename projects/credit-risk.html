Credit Risk Decisioning & Fairness Audit

A full credit decisioning system prioritizing calibration over ranking, with reject inference correcting for approval-only observed outcomes and fairness treated as an explicit policy tradeoff.

View on GitHub →

01The problem

A lending decision needs a true probability of default, not just a well-ranked score — expected loss is a function of a calibrated probability multiplied by exposure, and a model that ranks applicants correctly but reports overconfident probabilities produces systematically wrong loss estimates even when its AUC looks strong.

02The result

Headline number

Benchmarked gradient boosting against a traditional WOE-binned logistic scorecard on calibration quality specifically — Brier score, reliability diagrams, expected calibration error — rather than defaulting to whichever model had the higher AUC.

03Architecture

WOE scorecard vs. LightGBM → calibration diagnostics → reject inference on approval-only outcomes → fairness audit across protected groups → adverse-action reason codes → audit trail

04The hardest decision

Why calibration was prioritized over ranking metrics

AUC and similar ranking metrics answer 'does the model order applicants correctly' — they say nothing about whether a stated 8% default probability is actually an 8% real-world rate. Expected loss calculations need the second property, not the first. A model can have excellent AUC and be badly miscalibrated, producing confidently wrong loss estimates that look statistically sound until you check them against reality.

05What the results actually showed

Measured, not assumed

Outcomes are only observed for previously approved applicants — a structural selection bias that a naive model trained only on approved-applicant data inherits silently. Reject inference corrections were validated against simulated data specifically because the true outcome for a rejected applicant is, by definition, never observed in real data — there is no ground truth to check the correction against outside of a simulation built to contain one.

06What's next

Extend the fairness audit to intersectional subgroups rather than single protected attributes evaluated independently.
