Uplift Modeling & Causal Targeting Study

Heterogeneous treatment effect estimation on a randomized marketing trial, validated against simulated ground truth, with a placebo-test null result reported rather than shipped as a win.

View on GitHub →

01The problem

Standard propensity models identify customers likely to convert — not customers who convert because of an intervention. Targeting on propensity alone wastes spend on customers who would have converted regardless, and misses the customers an intervention would actually move.

02The result

Headline number

S-, T-, and X-learners plus a causal forest, each validated against simulated data with known individual treatment effects — since true counterfactuals are unobservable and no accuracy metric exists to check an uplift model against on real data alone.

03Architecture

randomized trial (n=64,000) → S/T/X-learners + causal forest → Qini curves, AUUC, decile uplift tables → placebo, covariate-balance, seed-stability tests → budget-constrained targeting policy

04The hardest decision

Why the placebo test mattered enough to report a null result

The placebo test — assigning a fake treatment and checking whether the model finds a spurious effect — showed the ranking was indistinguishable from noise on the primary arm. That's the test that actually tells you whether an uplift model is finding a real signal or an artifact of the estimator. Reporting it, rather than quietly moving on to a more flattering result, is what makes the rest of the analysis trustworthy.

05What the results actually showed

Measured, not assumed

18.6% of customers were flagged as negative-uplift — predicted to respond worse to the intervention — and that segment measured +0.44 percentage points against the randomized holdout when checked. A negative result, reported plainly rather than shipped as a targeting win, because a decision memo that hides its own estimator's failure mode is worse than useless to whoever has to act on it.

06What's next

Re-run with a larger trial to check whether the placebo-test noise floor shrinks with more data, or reflects a genuine ceiling on detectable heterogeneity in this population.
